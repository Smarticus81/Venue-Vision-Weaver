# Cinematic Wedding Video Prompts

A library of director-style prompts for AI wedding video generation. Each style targets a distinct visual identity and emotional tone. Use these as templates — the user’s couple image and venue images should be referenced naturally throughout the generated video.

-----

## STYLE 1: “The Cinematic Wedding Film” — Trailer Edition

A cinematic wedding trailer in the style of a Sundance-premiered short film, shot on ARRI Alexa Mini with anamorphic lenses, 2.39:1 aspect ratio, shallow depth of field, with natural lens flares and dreamy bokeh. Color grade: warm golden hour tones with soft pastel highlights, lifted shadows, slight film grain, Kodak Vision3 emulation. Frame rate: 24fps with selective 48fps slow-motion inserts.

**OPENING (0–3s):** Slow push-in through the venue’s grand entrance, soft morning light filtering through windows, dust motes suspended in beams of sun. Empty chairs in perfect rows, white florals trembling slightly in the breeze. Sound design implied: distant string quartet warming up, the hush before a held breath.

**RISING ACTION (3–8s):** Quick montage — the bride’s hand adjusting her veil, fingers grazing lace; the groom’s reflection in a mirror as he fastens his cufflinks, jaw tense with anticipation; guests arriving, embracing, laughter caught mid-motion. Handheld camera, intimate proximity, faces partially obscured then revealed.

**THE CEREMONY (8–14s):** Wide establishing shot of the ceremony space, every seat filled. Slow dolly-in on the couple at the altar, holding hands, foreheads nearly touching as vows are exchanged. Cut to tearful guests — a mother dabbing her eyes, the best man swallowing emotion. The kiss: shot from a low angle, backlit, silhouetted against soft window light, petals or confetti drifting in slow motion.

**THE CELEBRATION (14–22s):** Energy shift. Rapid cuts through the reception spaces — clinking champagne flutes catching candlelight, a long banquet table glowing with golden votives, guests mid-toast and mid-laugh. Whip pan to the dance floor: the couple’s first dance under string lights, twirling, the bride’s dress fanning out in slow motion. Cut to wild dancing — uncles with loosened ties, bridesmaids barefoot, a child spinning in circles. Camera moves with the crowd, immersive and kinetic.

**INTIMATE MOMENTS (22–27s):** Stolen glances. The couple alone for a moment on a balcony or quiet corridor of the venue, foreheads pressed together, whispering, laughing. A grandfather watching from across the room, smiling. Sparklers being lit, faces glowing orange in the dark.

**CLOSING (27–30s):** Final wide shot — the couple walking through a tunnel of sparklers or cheering guests, hand in hand, looking back once at the camera with a knowing smile. Slow fade. Last frame: the venue at night, windows glowing warm gold, the celebration still alive inside.

**TECHNICAL DIRECTION:** Prioritize natural skin tones, authentic emotion over staged poses, and architectural reverence for the venue — let the space breathe as a character. Avoid: oversaturation, plastic skin, uncanny smiles, symmetrical AI-face artifacts. Embrace: imperfect candid moments, motion blur on movement, depth and dimensionality in every frame.

**MOOD:** Joyful, tender, timeless. A love letter, not a commercial.

## STYLE 2: “Super 8 Memory” — Vintage Home Movie

A nostalgic wedding film shot on Super 8mm Kodak Ektachrome, 4:3 aspect ratio, 18fps with characteristic flicker and gate weave. Heavy film grain, faded warm tones with crushed blacks and blown highlights, occasional light leaks bleeding red and orange across frames. Slight motion blur, dust and hair-in-gate artifacts, dreamy soft focus on edges.

**OPENING:** A handheld pan across the venue exterior, sunlight flaring directly into the lens, the camera operator’s shadow briefly visible. The bride steps into frame, laughing, holding her bouquet up to shield her eyes from the sun.

**MIDDLE:** Disconnected, joyful fragments — the groom mid-laugh with his groomsmen, slightly out of focus; the couple feeding each other cake, frosting on a cheek; a flower girl spinning in circles on the dance floor; champagne being poured in slow, lingering shots. Every frame feels like a memory someone forgot they had.

**CEREMONY:** Shot from the back of the room, partially obscured by a guest’s shoulder. The kiss is brief, distant, but the cheering crowd fills the frame as the camera shakes with applause.

**CLOSING:** The couple running through a shower of rice or petals, the film stock flaring white as they pass the camera. Final frame: an empty champagne glass on a table, candlelight flickering, the film burning out to black with a soft pop.

**MOOD:** Warm, imperfect, achingly nostalgic. Like discovering your parents’ wedding reel in an attic.

**AVOID:** Digital sharpness, modern color science, stabilized footage, symmetrical compositions.

## STYLE 3: “Editorial Monochrome” — Vogue-Style Wedding

A high-fashion black-and-white wedding film in the style of Peter Lindbergh and Mario Sorrenti. Shot on medium format digital with vintage Leica Summilux glass, 1.85:1 aspect ratio. Pure monochrome with deep blacks, silvery midtones, and luminous highlights. Selective use of negative space, architectural framing, and chiaroscuro lighting. Slow, deliberate camera moves on a dolly or gimbal.

**OPENING:** Static wide shot of the venue’s most architecturally striking room — vaulted ceilings, marble, or rough-hewn stone — empty, light streaming through tall windows in hard geometric beams. The bride walks slowly into frame, her silhouette small against the architecture.

**PORTRAITURE:** Lingering close-ups treated as fashion editorial — the bride’s profile against a dark backdrop, light sculpting her cheekbone; the groom’s hands, weathered and steady, holding hers; eyes downcast, then up, direct to camera, unsmiling, then a slow break into a smile.

**CEREMONY:** Shot like a Caravaggio painting — single light source, deep shadows, guests as a chorus of faces in half-light. The vows are exchanged in profile, two silhouettes against a window.

**RECEPTION:** Dancing rendered in motion blur, bodies dissolving into abstraction. A laughing guest with their head thrown back, teeth catching the light. A still life of an emptied wine glass, a crumpled napkin, a wilted flower.

**CLOSING:** The couple alone in a vast empty room of the venue, dancing slowly. Wide shot, they are tiny in the frame. Slow fade to white.

**MOOD:** Timeless, sophisticated, emotionally restrained but deeply felt. Editorial gravity.

**AVOID:** Color tints, vintage filters, fast cuts, overt sentimentality.

## STYLE 4: “Symmetrical Storybook” — Wes Anderson Style

A wedding film composed in the meticulous style of Wes Anderson. Shot on 35mm with vintage Cooke lenses, 1.85:1 aspect ratio. Perfectly centered symmetrical compositions, flat staging, planimetric camera moves (whip pans, dolly-ins, overhead god’s-eye shots). Pastel color palette — dusty pinks, mustards, sage greens, powder blues — heightened and slightly desaturated, like a faded postcard.

**OPENING:** Overhead shot, perfectly flat. The venue laid out like a dollhouse — guests in their seats arranged with geometric precision, the aisle running dead-center. Title card appears in a serif font: “A Wedding.”

**CHARACTER INTRODUCTIONS:** Each member of the wedding party shown in a centered medium shot against a flat background, holding a prop (a bouquet, a champagne flute, a folded handkerchief). A small subtitle could appear: “The Bride.” “The Groom.” “The Mother of the Bride, Crying Already.”

**CEREMONY:** Shot dead-on from the aisle. The couple stands perfectly centered. A slow, mechanical dolly-in. Cut to a reverse shot of the guests — every face perfectly framed, expressions ranging from joy to mild bewilderment. The kiss happens in a single static wide.

**RECEPTION:** Whip pans between vignettes — an uncle delivering an unhinged toast, a child stealing cake, the couple’s hands clasped under the table. Overhead shot of the banquet table, food arranged like a still life. Dancing rendered in a single locked-off wide, guests entering and exiting frame in choreographed chaos.

**CLOSING:** The couple seated centered on a vintage sofa in a quiet room of the venue, looking directly at the camera, deadpan. A slow fade. End title: “Fin.”

**MOOD:** Whimsical, melancholic, precise. Funny without trying to be.

**AVOID:** Handheld camera, asymmetry, naturalistic blocking, improvised feeling.

## STYLE 5: “Golden Hour Dream” — Romantic Cinematic

A dreamlike wedding film bathed in perpetual golden hour, shot on Sony Venice with vintage Canon K35 lenses for soft, painterly bokeh. 2.39:1 aspect ratio, 60fps slow-motion throughout, drifting steadicam moves. Hazy backlight, sun flares blooming through every frame, warm honey tones, milky highlights, soft skin rendering. Everything feels suspended in amber.

**OPENING:** A field of wildflowers or a sun-soaked corridor of the venue, light raking sideways through windows or trees. The bride walks toward the camera in slow motion, her veil lifting in the breeze, laughing at something off-screen.

**THE COUPLE:** They run through the venue hand-in-hand, looking back at each other, never quite at the camera. He spins her. She buries her face in his shoulder. Every touch is in slow motion — fingers interlocking, a hand on the small of her back, a forehead kiss.

**CEREMONY:** Shot through gauzy fabric or out-of-focus florals in the foreground. The vows happen in a halo of backlight, faces softly glowing. Tears catch the light like small jewels.

**CELEBRATION:** Champagne arcing through the air in slow motion, droplets suspended. Dancers caught mid-twirl, dresses billowing. Sparklers traced through the dark like calligraphy. The couple’s first dance shot from a slow circular dolly, the world spinning around them.

**CLOSING:** Wide drone-style pull-back as the couple kisses on a balcony or hilltop at the venue, the sun setting behind them, the entire celebration glowing below. Slow fade to a single flickering candle.

**MOOD:** Pure romance, swooning, unapologetically beautiful. The wedding as a fairytale.

**AVOID:** Harsh shadows, cool color tones, fast cuts, documentary realism.

## STYLE 6: “Music Video Pulse” — Rhythmic & Kinetic

A high-energy wedding film cut to the pulse of an unheard beat, in the style of Spike Jonze and Hiro Murai. Shot on RED Komodo with modern Sigma Cine primes, 2.00:1 aspect ratio, mixed frame rates (24fps base, ramping into 120fps slow-motion and snapping back). Bold, saturated color grade with teal-and-orange undertones, deep contrast, neon highlights at night. Aggressive camera movement: gimbal whip-pans, dutch angles, push-ins on the beat.

**OPENING:** A single, sustained close-up on the bride’s eyes opening — slow zoom out as she breathes in. Beat drop. Hard cut to the venue exterior in a sweeping drone shot, the building revealed in one continuous move.

**GETTING READY:** Match cuts on rhythm — a cufflink snapping shut cuts to a heel clicking on marble cuts to a champagne cork popping cuts to a door swinging open. Every cut lands on an implied beat. Quick handheld whips between the bride’s suite and the groom’s suite, building parallel tension.

**THE CEREMONY:** Slow-motion walk down the aisle, the bride’s face in tight close-up, every micro-expression visible. Cut to the groom’s face — a held breath, then a smile breaking. The kiss hits on a sound design swell, then sudden silence as the camera circles them 360 degrees.

**RECEPTION:** This is where the film unleashes. Rapid-fire cuts through every room of the venue — a hand grabbing a drink, feet hitting the dance floor, a laugh in close-up, confetti exploding in 120fps. The camera weaves through the crowd in long oner shots, finding faces, losing them, finding the couple again. Strobe-like flashes on the dance floor, bodies frozen mid-motion. A guest screaming the lyrics to a song we can’t hear.

**THE BREAKDOWN:** A sudden quiet. Slow-motion shot of the couple in the middle of the dance floor, everyone else blurred around them. They look at each other like no one else exists. Hold. Hold. Hold.

**FINALE:** The beat returns. Everyone jumping in unison, hands in the air, the couple lifted by friends. Final shot: the couple running out of the venue at night, sparklers exploding behind them, freeze frame as they leap into the air, mid-laugh.

**MOOD:** Electric, alive, unforgettable. A wedding as a music video as a memory you’ll replay forever.

**AVOID:** Static compositions, soft pastel grading, sentimentality, slow pacing without payoff.

## Usage Notes

- **Couple image input:** Reference the couple’s likeness consistently across all scenes — same faces, same hair, same general styling, but allow for natural wardrobe changes (ceremony attire vs. reception attire vs. getting-ready looks).
- **Venue image inputs:** Treat the venue as a character. Pull architectural details, lighting conditions, and spatial flow from the reference images. Different rooms should feel like different acts in the film.
- **Length:** Each prompt is calibrated for a 25–35 second cinematic trailer. For longer outputs, expand the celebration and intimate moments sections.
- **Customization hooks:** Each style can be lightly modified with seasonal cues (autumn leaves, winter snow, spring blossoms), cultural traditions (specific ceremonies, dances, attire), or time-of-day shifts (sunrise ceremony, twilight reception, midnight sparkler send-off).
